# Proposed Design v1

## Chosen design

### Approach

The proposed design is **approval-preserving attention engineering**: the approval decision remains a human decision for every deployment, on the unchanged gate, and everything built around it re-engineers how the fixed reviewer pool's attention is classified, prepared, routed, batched, and measured. This is the framing's Reading 4 ("faster informed human decisions via tiering, routing, batching, better pre-review evidence — without touching the approval concept") developed jointly with the usable half of Idea 3 (assistance), while its unusable halves — verdict-shaped recommendation and default-approve-with-a-window — are rejected. It is selected as the common trunk of the solution family: the classify-and-auto-approve direction is not discarded but recorded as the specified, security-gated branch that grows from the data this design produces (see Automation on-ramp).

The commitments that define the direction, stated so the next stage can challenge them:

1. **No deployment decision is automated, by mechanism or by creep.** No classifier output authorizes a deployment, no qualification bypasses the gate, no windowed default converts silence into consent, no batch signature covers items, no lapse implies approval. One deployment, one human decision, one named sign-off, one record. The disqualifying line is bright and structural, not advisory.
2. **Classification is advice about attention, never a claim about safety.** The tierer says "this change is routine-shaped; a reviewer can confirm it in minutes" or "this touches judgment territory; here is where." It never asserts low-risk or dangerous — both are claims about a deployment's fate, and the fate has exactly one arbiter: the human. This resolves the framing's explicitly unmade consumer question (D15, Q3) as the design's defining choice: **the machine prepares, the human decides quickly.** Classification errors here are attention-allocation errors, which is why the design needs no answer to Q1 to run.
3. **The decision unit is the deployment, always.** Batching is a presentation and scheduling mechanism, never a decision mechanism. Guarantees (a) "a human has looked at every change" and (b) "a recorded named sign-off" survive untouched in per-change form, and (c) the named-accountability guarantee needs no new machinery: the approver is the accountable party because a decision-act exists for every deployment. The sponsor's D14 default ("policy evaluation counts as the decision") is moot — there is no policy-only deployment, so the unauditable-non-decision problem the automation directions must engineer around never arises.
4. **Assistance, not recommendation.** The brief presents what the change is (mechanically derived), what is verified and by whom, what is unverified, and what the depth policy says the reviewer's attention should be. It never proposes approve/deny: a verdict-shaped suggestion is the brief's information content minus the reasoning plus an anchoring term, and it is precisely where automation bias does its work. "Approve" and "deny" remain human acts alone.
5. **The gate is untouched; the design is an overlay in front of it.** The pipeline's approval event is the same event, made by the same humans, recorded the same way — plus instrumentation. Q1 (security's position on automation) never gates this design, Q4 ("no side channel" interpretation for class-differentiated gates) never arises — there are no class-differentiated gates, only differentiated queues in front of one gate — and Q5 and Q6 have no antecedent.
6. **The design has no dangerous-false-negative class of its own.** A tiering error wastes or misallocates attention; it cannot release a change. The worst operational failure is today's gate with today's queue. The one direction of error with a safety character — mis-tiering a consequential change into shallow batch review (F1) — is admitted, bounded by conservative conjunction and measured escalation, and named rather than euphemized.
7. **Re-engineered attention becomes measured, auditable signal.** Every review leaves a record of what the human was shown (brief version, content-addressed), what depth policy allotted, what they did with it (disposition, tier disagreement, time), and a named sign-off. This delivers the framing's baseline instrumentation commitment (D5) — denials, reworks, incident attribution made queryable — plus a review-context dimension no other direction records for human decisions. Attention quality, the framing's own success metric, becomes an observable.
8. **The ceiling is stated by the design, not discovered by its critics.** The per-change human look cannot be engineered below roughly a minute-scale confirmation, and the batch cadence imposes a floor on routine wait. This design does not remove the queue from the deployer's path; it may not reduce hours much, and it may add modest mean wait for the routine class. The framing accepted exactly this when it endorsed Reading 4; the design takes that decision at its word, measures the latency effect (E5), and does not pretend past it.

**What is adopted from the classification proposals, with credit.** The following are direction-independent requirements of the framing's audit bar and are adopted wholesale rather than re-derived: evidence provenance classes (D13), snapshot-only deterministic evaluation with a content-addressed evaluator, the conjunctive predicate set, artifact-hash binding ("the thing the reviewer is shown describes the thing that ran"), append-only record stores, disposition capture as first-class records, and the never-self rule (in advisory form: the never-T1 set). The difference is what the evaluation feeds: a screen, not a gate.

### System primitives

**Evidence object.** `{id, kind, value, source-class, origin, capture-time, integrity, derived-from[]}`, `source-class ∈ {author-supplied, pipeline-derived, pipeline-observed}`, bound by hash to the deployment artifact. Provenance classes remain load-bearing here for a different reason than in the automation designs: the brief is what a human reads, so a laundered author claim no longer risks an automated decision — it risks misdirecting the one resource the direction conserves, which is attention. Author-supplied evidence cannot satisfy a safety-relevant predicate; a fact only the author asserts forces a higher tier, where the unverified assertion is displayed for exactly the human attention it should attract.

**Attention tier.** A deterministic, conjunctive classification assigning every change to exactly one tier. **T1 (batch-screened)** — every predicate affirms on independently generated or pipeline-observed evidence: `artifact-integrity` (evaluated artifact ≡ deployed artifact, pipeline-attested); `composition-bounded` (change composition mechanically derived from the diff falls in a bounded set — declared type is never an input); `blast-radius-bounded` (affected services from the pipeline-maintained graph within the policy's bounded set); `failure-detectable` (monitoring coverage exists for the change class's failure-mode family); `tests-affirmative` (relevant tests pass; coverage not degraded); `no-special-infra` (diff touches none of: feature flags, traffic routing, security settings, credentials, migrations, infrastructure code); `history-clean` (no open incident on affected services; actor/service cadence below thresholds). **T3 (deep review)** — special infrastructure, wide blast radius, open incident, or the never-T1 set. **T2 (individual judgment)** — the default, everything else. Untiered is defined as not-T1: degradation conserves attention. T1 starts conservative — the mechanically provable core (content, docs, attested pinned-image bumps) — and expands only on measured escalation quiet. Over-inclusion in T1 misroutes depth (a human still decides); over-inclusion in T3 wastes attention; only the first direction is safety-relevant, so T1 is deliberately conservative.

**Attention policy.** The versioned, owned document: tiering rules, the **depth rubric** (the tier-by-tier contract for how deep review goes — the checklist and expected investment per class), and the routing matrix (which reviewer pools own which domains at which tiers). Deliberately and loudly **not an approval policy**: it governs allocation of a fixed human resource, not admission to production. A wrong clause costs attention; a permissive clause is bounded by the fact that a human always decides. It is versioned (content-addressed, named human owner, changes separately approved per D10) because audit must be able to say which allocation rules were in force when a change was presented — but its governance is lighter than gate-policy governance, and that lightness is a named, defended surface (F5).

**Review brief.** The pre-digested reviewer screen, generated deterministically from the frozen snapshot: per-rule outcomes with source-class visible on every input; a structured diff summary (never raw diff scrolling); blast-radius summary; monitoring-coverage summary; test summary; the tier and its trace; the **depth rubric** for this class; and the history of similar changes in this service/class (recent dispositions, common denial reasons, rework rate). Content-addressed and versioned; disposition records store its fingerprint and generator version so "what was the reviewer actually shown" is replayable. The same brief is shown to the deployer at enqueue — the deployer sees the presentation their review will receive, which aligns incentives toward the scrutiny the change will face.

**Batch abstract.** Metadata for a T1 session: grouping key (domain, class, or both), commonality summary (what is identical across the batch — read once), and **outlier marks** (items differing from the group in any safety-relevant dimension, flagged for full opening). A batch is a presentation vehicle; it is never a decision unit. The abstract records per-item sign-off status so a batch cannot close with an unsigned item.

**Disposition record.** The superset of today's approval log: `{decision-id, disposition ∈ {approved, denied, reworked, escalated}, approver, timestamp, tier-at-presentation, brief-fingerprint, escalation?, annotations[]}`. Annotations let the reviewer anchor a denial to specific brief rules ("denied on `history-clean`: open incident INC-4471 on `payments`") — this is the framing's baseline instrumentation (D5) made into a reviewer tool, and the anchored reason is the record that makes the reviewer's screening reasoning queryable for the first time.

**Escalation record.** A reviewer-initiated re-tier with a structured reason: the design's divergence signal — a human disagreeing with where attention was routed. Escalation patterns feed attention-policy revision exactly as shadow divergences would feed policy revision in the automation designs, at attention's stakes rather than admission's. **This is also this design's adoption of the policy-replaces proposal's encodability insight, harvested as a byproduct**: anchored dispositions and escalation confirmations measure, continuously and freely, whether reviewer judgment reduces to rules — the encodability signal the policy-replaces design bills as a classified-labor tax (its divergence classification loop) falls out of ordinary review here.

**Review-context record.** `{deployment-id, brief-version, tier, tier-agreement ∈ {none, concur, disputed}, reviewer-time, disposition, reason, sign-off-name, timestamp}` — appended to the existing approval record; the sign-off is the existing sign-off. This makes attention auditable: for every deployment one can ask what the human was shown, at what allotted depth, and what they did with it.

**Attention ledger.** The standing measurement of the framing's redefined success: time-to-review by tier, queue depth and staleness, batch cadence planned vs. achieved, per-item sign-off times, rework and denial rates by class, escalation rates by rule and outcome, incident attribution by tier, flagged-but-enqueued rate (the social-gate watch, F7). Because "attention quality" has no naive operationalization, the design proposes proxies and names them as proxies: anchored-denial share (denials citing specific rules — measurably more informative review than free text), escalation confirmations (do escalated items, on re-review, produce denials/rework — did deeper scrutiny find what the batch would have missed), time-per-item against rubric expectation, and incident attribution by tier.

### Architecture

Four components, one trust domain, **no actuators**. This absence is the architecture's central statement: nothing in the pipeline responds to the tier with changed control flow. The gate executes byte-for-byte as today in every phase.

**Evidence preparation** (this team) — snapshot assembly, provenance conversion, blast-radius graph consumption. Adopted from the shared machinery. **Brief and tier generator** (this team) — a deterministic, versioned function `(snapshot, attention-policy-version, generator-digest) → {tier, trace, brief, batch-abstract}`. Determinism requirements adopted (no wall clock, no evaluation-time I/O, content-addressed generator, replay-regression over the disposition corpus gating every generator release) because the brief is the artifact a human decides on and "what was the reviewer shown" must be reconstructible. Strictly cheaper than the automation designs' evaluator: the function prepares a view and decides nothing, so a generator bug can mislead attention but cannot admit a deployment, and the regression gate sizes to that. **Queue and triage presentation** (platform team's approval UI) — tier display, queue structures, batch session construction, escalation capture, SLA visibility, disposition capture. This is the entire platform dependency: a view and a recorder, with no mode controller, no bypass, no live/shadow pair. **Disposition store and measurement loop** (this team) — append-only, write-restricted to the review UI's disposition hook; the ledger, escalation divergence reports, SLA dashboards.

**Integrity.** The brief binds to the artifact hash and the screen attests "this brief describes the artifact that built from this commit" — the evaluate-clean-view-dirty attack is killed at the presentation layer. If binding fails, the reviewer gets the raw view and a notice: review proceeds, undegraded, on the honest materials.

**Degradation is the steady state, not a fallback.** Generator down → untiered queue, no batches, raw screen: exactly today. Brief buggy → briefs suppressed with a notice: exactly today. Policy lapsed → conservative defaults: exactly today. The direction's failure surface is literally a return to the status quo, because the status quo is the substrate the direction decorates, not something it replaces.

**Phasing.** *P0* — disposition capture with reasons, review-context hook, incident-attribution linkage: the platform team's smallest ask (a data write, a field on the existing approval screen), doubling as the baseline measurement every direction inherits from D5. In parallel, reviewer interviews begin (soft-gated, Implementation constraints). *P1* — recorded tiers: the generator runs; reviewers see tier, trace, and brief alongside the unchanged screen; routing and batching not yet live. Escalations and dispositions accumulate. This tuning loop is deliberately **not called a shadow period**: there is no cutover downstream, no parity criterion, no oracle being validated for authorization. The loop tunes routing quality. *P2* — routing and batching presentation, per domain, feature-flagged and measured against the P0 baseline: batch sessions for T1, domain-default routing for T2/T3, SLA dashboards live before any commitment is made. The per-domain graduation gate is escalation-rate asymmetry below a queue-ops-set threshold over minimum volume per class — an attention-quality gate, not a safety gate; per-feature revert is one deploy. *P3* — steady state: attention-policy revision cadence, brief-feedback review, measurement audits.

### User workflows

**Deployer.** Push → pipeline → evidence prep and tier generation with the other gates. Before enqueue, the deployer sees the **enqueue screening**: the tier, the mechanically derived check summary, and the brief. A mechanically failing change is visible now, anchored the way the reviewer will see it — before a reviewer spends twenty minutes reconstructing and denying it. The screening is non-binding: no block, no override, no new path; the deployer may enqueue anyway and the flags ride in the brief (and the flagged-but-enqueued rate is measured, F7). On enqueue, routing follows tier: **T1** joins the next batch session for its domain, with a review-slot commitment ("next routine batch, expected within N hours") where N is set from measured cadence, never aspiration. **T2** joins the visible pool queue with position and staleness shown. **T3** routes to the named accountable reviewer pool for the affected domain, with interrupt-appropriate alerting — T3 arrivals are events, not queue items. During review the deployer sees progress (which reviewer claimed the item, how far through the brief), and a denial arrives stamped with the specific rules and evidence that failed — the resubmission references the prior disposition and the second review opens with a diff-against-prior-brief rather than a cold screen. Pre-deploy transparency (Q8) is answered yes, for the adopted reason: the boundary is learnable by probing regardless, and gameability is fought at evidence provenance, not opacity.

**Reviewer.** Three modes replace one undifferentiated mode:

| Tier | Presentation | Evidence depth | Norm | Sign-off |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| T1 | Batch session, pre-scheduled, calendar-reserved, bounded cohort size | Commonality summary once; per-item brief; outlier-marked items opened in full | Shallow-but-present confirmation on strong prepared evidence; every item individually confirmed | Per-item named sign-off, one confirmation per brief — never a batch-level sign-off |
| T2 | Visible pool, oldest-first default, domain defaults | Full brief; raw drill-down one click away | Individual judgment, as today but evidence-prepped | Named sign-off, recorded as always |
| T3 | Routed to named domain reviewers, scheduled deep-review blocks | Full brief plus full raw evidence; no summarization shortcuts | Unhurried deep review; the framing already accepted this cohort may get slower | Named sign-off |

Per-item sign-off in the T1 row is structural: batching the presentation must never batch the sign-off, because guarantees (b) and (c) are per-deployment. The UI enforces it by shape — a batch cannot close with an unsigned item, and the cohort summary is a report, not a signature. Two affordances matter beyond the table: **escalation** (one click per item bumps the tier with a structured reason; escalated items carry the note to the receiving reviewer; escalation is a first-class tracked act whose corpus drives policy revision) and **anchored dispositions** (denials citing specific rules, making reasoning queryable). The only new reviewer actions are disposition reasons (P0, shared by every direction), tier flags and brief flags (one click each) — **there is no sampled re-review tax**: every change already receives full human review, so the review is the measurement.

**Queue operations lead.** A new, operationally real role drawn from existing reviewer seniority: owns routing defaults, batch cadence calibrated against measured reviewer availability, SLA commitments, escalation review, and the standing tiering retro where escalation patterns and disposition outcomes decide whether T1 expands or contracts. This formalizes queue coordination that today happens implicitly (or not at all — part of the rubber-stamping story); the design states the staffing need rather than pretending coordination is free.

**Attention policy owner.** Owns the tiering rules, depth rubric, routing matrix, and version history. Lighter than the automation designs' policy-owner role in exactly the ways that matter: accountable for allocation quality (measured by escalation divergence), **not** per-deployment accountable on every change that transits under their rules. Security co-ownership (D10's proposal) is optional here, not a precondition.

**Security.** The role shifts from licensor to reader: disposition records, escalation rates, brief-audit reports, incident attribution by tier, and the flagged-but-enqueued watch. Optionally, a standing veto over attention-policy diffs. Security time is consumed nowhere on the critical path — no threshold must be set, no capacity must be secured, no inspection license must be granted before any phase ships. The reports this design produces are the homework security needs for the Q1 conversation whenever they have it.

**Incident responder.** Attribution resolves through the disposition chain: deployment → reviewer → brief version → tier → reasons. Incident review can now ask — and the record can answer — the two questions the rubber-stamping regime could not formulate: *was this change presented accurately* (replay the brief from its fingerprint) and *was it presented at the right depth* (tier vs. what the change turned out to be). No other direction's records answer the second question about their residual human gates; this one answers it about every deployment.

### Failure modes

**F1. Mis-tiering into T1 — a consequential change batch-screened shallow.** The central risk, stated without euphemism: not a false negative on a decision (a human decided; the gate held) but a misrouting of scrutiny depth, and the one place this direction can make the screening picture worse than status quo in kind, because T1 review is deliberately shallow where today's uniform review is uniformly whatever-depth. Mitigations: T1 admission is a conjunction, never a score (a score buys off scrutiny depth, exactly wrong here); batch construction marks outliers and the session norm requires opening them; escalation is one click and its confirmation rate is the standing T1-expansion gate; conservative start and expansion only on measured escalation quiet; incident attribution by tier queries whether batch-screened changes are overrepresented. Residual: scrutiny depth is a property of human behavior; the design makes depth allocation visible, instrumented, and revisable rather than uniformly unexamined — it cannot machine-enforce depth. That residual is also the status quo's, unmeasured today and measured under this design.

**F2. Batch blur.** Homogeneous items produce complacency; the different item gets the group's coat of paint. Mitigations: bounded sessions, homogeneity grouping built around surfacing the odd item, per-item sign-off as structural UI fact. Residual: a reviewer who confirms without looking is undetectable by any machinery — true of every human gate, and the framing's rubber-stamping premise, bounded not eliminated.

**F3. Queue starvation and cherry-picking.** Mitigations: staleness bins with escalation thresholds, domain routing giving every change a default owner so "nobody is looking" has a name, oldest-first defaults. All defaults, none enforceable; the failure this cannot fully kill is social, and the design says so instead of building permission machinery.

**F4. Routing hardens into a shadow gate.** Presentation defaults accrete into de facto permissions; a change stuck in an unowned-looking quadrant waits on a gate nobody signed off on — this direction's version of the side-channel constraint. Mitigation: the invariant *routing is default-presentation, never filtering* is written into the attention policy and checked in platform UI review ("can any reviewer see and claim any item? if not, this is a gate and needs the gate's governance").

**F5. Attention-policy erosion — the dial problem relocated.** The classify proposal warned that thresholds "feel operational rather than governance-grade"; this design's deliberately lighter policy governance is exposed to the same drift — a quietly loosened T1, a broadened batch session, and depth erodes without any decision ever being automated. The counter is not heavy governance but heavy telemetry: every attention-policy change publishes its predicted routing-distribution impact before release and is checked against measured escalation and disposition shifts after; a T1 expansion that produces an escalation spike is reverted by policy revision, as cheaply as it was made. Light governance with loud telemetry is the designed trade; F5 is the named attack on it.

**F6. Brief rot.** Mitigations adopted: deterministic generation with replay-regression gating generator releases (cheaper than the automation designs' version — the brief decides nothing); provenance display on every input; a brief-feedback channel where reviewers — the brief's consumers and best critics — file "this brief was misleading" reports that feed the revision loop.

**F7. The enqueue flag becomes a de facto gate.** Mitigation is measurement: the flagged-but-enqueued rate and the denial rate among those enqueued are ledger metrics; a collapse toward zero is an alarm that the social gate has formed. Communication and ownership, treated as such.

**F8. SLA theater.** The rule: the SLA is set from measured cadence, never before it; the dashboard ships before the commitment does; a missed SLA is an operational incident in the ledger.

**F9. The measurement loop lies.** Adopted: an unmeasured measurement system quietly invalidates the escalation-divergence gate. Lighter here — nothing downstream is a cutover decision — but the escalation-rate threshold gating T1 expansion inherits the audit obligation.

**F10. Adoption failure — reviewers do not batch.** If the pool declines batch sessions, grabs ad hoc, and treats the brief as decoration, the design collapses to exactly the advisory-only variant the framing dismissed as inert. This is the design's only true dependency and it is social: the reallocation of attention does not happen in software. Mitigations are calendar-reserved sessions, visible outcomes (escalation stats, anchored dispositions showing the brief working), and a success metric (D2) that makes adoption the goal — but the dependency is named because naming it is the only honest treatment.

### Operational complexity

The headline is a structural fact: **this is the cheapest of the three proposals to operate.** One decision verb, one actor class, one accountability shape; no live/shadow mode pair, no override machinery, no objection channel, no certificate semantics, no terminology migration program (the policy-replaces design correctly identifies vocabulary migration as "a program, not a rename" — this direction does not change the concept, so it does not pay for it), and degradation that is the status quo rather than a designed fallback. The runbook has one line: "overlay down → untiered queue, raw screen, same gate."

The real standing costs, named with their owners: **platform UI work** — the direction's entire enforcement-adjacent surface, incremental by phase (P0 disposition field, P1 screen augmentation, P2 queue-presentation rework), and a view change on an unchanged gate rather than a gate change; **queue-operations staffing** — the new named role above, drawn from existing reviewer seniority; and **the batch calendar** — scheduled sessions consuming reviewer calendar in deliberate blocks, a change-management cost that is the point: attention reallocation does not happen in software, and the design budgets the rollout of that adoption as an activity, not an assumption. Standing machinery otherwise mirrors the shared trunk (evidence ingestion, generator, stores, measurement loop) at lower feed pressure than the automation designs: replay is audit-demand-driven, no security-facing one-command-replay as a safety dependency, no cutover ceremony.

**Latency honesty, as an operational property.** The batch cadence imposes a floor on T1 wait; a change arriving just after a session waits for the next. Batching trades mean wait for predictability, warm context, and fewer rework re-entries — the net effect on mean queue delay is *measurable, not predictable ex ante*, and it is plausible this direction mildly increases mean wait for the routine class. Under D2 (attention quality; hours saved "a proxy, not the goal") that trade is accepted as written, and the design records rather than hides that this is the one axis where it may be net-negative against the seed's original complaint of deployer delay.

**Reviewer-hour accounting.** New reviewer seconds: disposition reasons (P0 — charged once, to every direction, since D5 demands the instrumentation regardless), tier flags, brief flags. The sampled re-review tax the classify design bills as deliberate and security-set does not exist here, because the review is the measurement. The policy-replaces design bills representational audits, matrix sign-offs, and encodability classification against the fixed pool; this design's analogues are the brief audit (a fraction of one person, mechanical plus sampling) and queue management (rotation).

### Security implications

**The automation constraint is inert.** "Any automated approval must be inspectable after the fact by the security team" — there is no automated approval, so there is nothing to license and no Q1 dependency anywhere in the delivery path. Both automation directions declare their live modes conditional on the security conversation's answer; this design's entire value is unconditional on it. For a framing whose confidence is dominated by exactly that unresolved question, that is the security posture in one sentence.

**The security surface is the allocation, not the decision.** The tiering rules are where the safety boundary passes through this direction: a mis-tier routes scrutiny depth, and depth is a screening-quality input. The security-relevant artifacts are therefore the attention policy (published, diff-visible, veto available), the T1 admission predicate set (conservative by construction), the escalation divergence reports, and the one genuinely new capability: **incident attribution by tier**, which lets security ask whether the batch-screened class is overrepresented in incidents and demand a T1 contraction if it is. The direction invites this scrutiny — its safety case strengthens the more security looks at the allocation data, because the claim "attention reallocation improves screening quality" is falsifiable with the disposition and attribution records now in hand.

**The honest residual, stated by the design: the A5 exposure.** The entire safety increment rides on the framing's assumption A5 — "attention quality serves screening quality" — which the framing itself flags as "plausible and consistent with the answers, but not yet evidenced." This direction has no machine-checkable safety boundary of its own; it is attention engineering on top of the unchanged human gate. If A5 is false, the design retains "no worse than unchanged" — which satisfies the framing's safety-parity requirement, but forfeits the safety upside. The mitigation is that A5 is testable in the design's own P0 data (disposition records plus review-context times) before any queue change ships: whether shallow-screening patterns correlate with missed denials. If the test fails, the automation branch's evidence picture changes and the record should say so (E1).

**Brief gameability is fought at evidence provenance, at lower stakes than the automation designs.** Author-supplied claims cannot satisfy tiering predicates; tier and brief derive mechanically. A shaped change that reads well in a brief still faces a human who can drill down, escalate, and deny with a recorded, anchored reason — and the deployer sees the same brief at enqueue, so optimizing the change toward the presentation optimizes it toward the scrutiny. Payoff for gaming is a shorter line, not a bypassed human. The **never-T1 set** (tiering code, policy corpus, pipeline control, security-critical configuration structurally excluded from T1) is the rollback self-application rule in advisory form — and the framing's rollout paradox itself shrinks to near-triviality here: this mechanism does not alter the fate of any future deployment, so its own deployment is a normal-risk change through the unchanged human gate.

**Adopted hygiene.** Secrets-in-records: predicates read derived structure only, raw diffs retained under access control. Disposition and review-context stores append-only, write-restricted to the disposition hook. The tiering service has no actuator: its compromise is a presentation-layer compromise, recoverable by feature flag — a category of incident the automation designs' gate machinery can have and this design cannot.

### Implementation constraints

**Platform-team dependency, stacked smallest-to-largest, each phase valuable alone.** P0: a disposition-capture field and review-context hook on the existing approval screen — the baseline instrumentation D5 already commits to, not a cost this direction uniquely imposes. P1: display the tier and brief on that screen. P2: batch construction, routing defaults, staleness surfaces — the largest ask, a queue-presentation rework, still a view change on the unchanged gate. Q11 (the platform team's change process) applies to all of it; the position is that the weight of the ask is a separate variable from its existence, and this is the lightest ask of the three directions by a class. Crucially, there is a degradation floor the automation designs lack: if the platform team refuses UI integration, this design degrades to a sidecar mode — briefs rendered in an adjacent tool, reviewer keeps two windows — degraded, worse, but operational, with the gate untouched.

**Determinism is a build-time property of the brief generator**, for the replay-grade audit reason: "what the reviewer was shown" must be reconstructible. No policy language, no totality proof over a decision function — a versioned, content-addressed generator with replay-regression over the disposition corpus gating releases. This discipline is non-negotiable from the first commit, because a non-replayable brief is an unauditable input to the sole control.

**Queueing honesty is a constraint.** SLAs are set from measured batch cadence, set from measured reviewer availability. The design forbids promising a review-slot commitment before its dashboard exists — broken predictability is worse than none (F8).

**Inherited open questions, sorted.** *Inert for this direction:* Q1 (nothing to license), Q4 in its strong form (no differentiated gate), Q5 (no policy-only deployment exists), Q6 (no machine-decision FNR; the escalation-rate gate is a queue-ops attention parameter, not a security threshold). *Live, weakened:* Q2 — the T1 criteria values remain criteria-work, but a wrong value buys misallocation, not authorization, so values can start conservative-and-small and expand on escalation evidence rather than blocking operation; a missing value fails a rule to a higher tier. Q8 — answered: full deployer transparency, defended at provenance. Q9 — relocated: an attention-policy owner and a queue-ops lead are required before P2, drawn from the existing fixed pool, lighter-gauge than the automation designs' roles and not a live-mode gate. Q11 — live in weakest form. *Untouched:* Q7 — the emergency-revert gap remains the organization's open item; this direction adds no override surface and changes nothing about the revert path. *Non-blocking:* Q10 — the design compiles no guarantees into rules, so it does not need the list complete; the P0 reviewer interviews advance Q10 as a side effect.

**Staffing minimally required:** P0–P1 run with this team alone plus the platform team's field; P2 requires the named attention-policy owner and queue-ops lead from reviewer seniority. Security staffing required for delivery: none. This is the only direction of the three deliverable on the org chart that exists this quarter.

### Automation on-ramp (specified branch, not adopted now)

This design is chosen as the common trunk, and the compaction records the branch so the on-ramp decision is not re-litigated later. The P0/P1 machinery — evidence ingestion, the conjunctive predicate evaluator, deterministic snapshot evaluation, disposition capture — is exactly the front half of the classification designs, and the disposition-plus-escalation records are the labels a future classifier needs. The reverse conversion does not hold: automation machinery built first would not cleanly convert back to this design.

The specified branch is **classify-and-auto-approve in its decision-record-first form**: deterministic conjunctive policy evaluation with `LOW-RISK`/`REVIEW` outcomes, the machine affirming low risk only (never asserting danger), overrides as decision records on the one path (never a separate channel), D14's default semantics — the policy evaluation counts as the decision with named accountable parties (policy owner plus deployer) — and the full audit bar (policy version pinned, evidence with provenance, evaluation trace, replays). The policy-replaces-approval direction is recorded as rejected for the branch as well (see below); its encodability insight survives as this design's escalation/anchoring instrumentation, as noted.

The branch's entry conditions are the framing's own gates, now with an honest chance of being satisfiable: Q1 answered by security against a measured baseline (D5 — the baseline measurement this design's P0 delivers is the precondition for any survival claim); Q2 criteria values owned; Q6 threshold security-set against that baseline, not a guess; Q9 policy owner assigned. Until then, classification runs in recording mode (P1), stays in recording mode, and costs nothing but compute. The weak-oracle problem both automation proposals concede in their shadow epistemics — a zero-divergence streak cannot distinguish "agrees because correct" from "agrees because equally rubber" — is addressed by the only honest means: the branch's cutover evidence is evaluated against the measured, instrumented baseline this design produces, with the classified-by-human divergence and dangerous-class labels captured along the way. The on-ramp argument is a dependency argument: the branch needs a license nobody has asked for and data nobody has collected; this design needs neither, and produces the second while the first is being asked.

## Why this design was chosen

**Against the priority order:**

1. *Safety and security governs.* Both automation designs pay for their throughput with a modification of guarantee (a) — the classify design calls it "the concession the reading makes," the policy-replaces design marks g1 and g2 `modified` in its own matrix, preserving intact only the guarantee the sponsor added because it was expressible in compiled form. They make that payment on the strength of a license (Q1) that has not been asked for. Under the priority order, modifying a named guarantee before the security team has spoken inverts the first priority. This design preserves all three guarantees unmodified in per-change form, cannot fail open, and its central risk (shallow T1 screening) is admitted, bounded by a conservative conjunction, and corrected by a one-click escalation whose confirmation rate is the standing gate on T1 expansion. The automation directions' safety case, by contrast, rests at its weakest point on agreement with a rubber-stamping oracle they themselves concede cannot certify their decision-maker.
2. *Reliability and operability.* Degradation here is byte-for-byte the status quo; the sole control's availability has no dependency on the overlay. The automation directions insert machinery into the decision path — a mode controller satisfying the gate for the classify design, a stage bypass for the policy-replaces design — before the platform team (whose change process gates any such change, Q11) is even in the room. A design whose operability depends on a permissive answer to a question nobody has asked has ordered its dependencies badly; this one does not ask.
3. *Developer velocity.* The cost is taken here, deliberately. The framing's own decisions already accept subordinated velocity: the shadow period and baseline-first sequencing delay the throughput win for *every* direction, and the success metric is attention quality, not hours (D2). The chosen design optimizes the framing's actual metric directly, and converts the mandatory delay into the data-production phase every direction requires anyway — P0 instrumentation is D5, shared. The measured queue data (E5) then decides empirically whether the velocity gap is real enough to pursue the automation branch, instead of betting a named guarantee on it today.
4. *Convenience.* Spent deliberately: one-keystroke per-item dispositions make the per-deployment decision act near-free precisely so no efficiency argument exists for batching the sign-off. Priority 4 is consumed to defend priorities 1–2.

**Against the framing's constraints:** every deployment decision remains auditable — satisfied and enriched (the decision existed all along; the record now captures what was shown and at what depth). "All deployments go through the shared pipeline, no side channel" — trivially satisfied because no differentiated gate exists; only differentiated queues in front of one gate, which is why Q4 never arises. Fixed reviewer capacity — reallocated, with near-zero added tax: the review is the measurement, where the classify design bills a deliberate sampled re-review tax and the policy-replaces design bills representational audits and encodability classification, from pools the framing records as fixed and unsecured. "Automated approval inspectable after the fact" — vacuous: none exists, and the specified branch's inspectability bar is already recorded (replay-grade decision records per the classify architecture).

**Why this wins over the others.** Over classify-and-auto-approve, the strongest competitor: it is the same machinery's front half run without the license; its live mode is conditional on five unanswered items (Q1, Q4, Q2 values, Q6, Q9); it modifies guarantee (a); and its cutover epistemics are its own named weakness. Its decision-record primitives and determinism discipline are adopted here and preserved as the specified branch — the choice is sequencing, and the data this design produces is exactly what turns the branch's assumptions into measurements. Over policy-replaces-approval: it cannot define its certificate's claim semantics without a ratified guarantee list (Q10, unreachable until the approvers are asked), its accountability "as practiced may be nominal" on decisions nobody actuated (its own F2), it self-reports as the heaviest consumer of unsecured security time (its own D6), and its platform ask is the sharpest possible Q4 reading (bypass, its own I2). Its one genuinely valuable methodology — measuring whether human judgment is encodable — is adopted here at a fraction of the cost, as the escalation-and-anchoring instrumentation. The selection is also rooted in the framing's own record: D16 records the sponsor's suspicion that uniform application is the root cause, and the framing endorses Reading 4 as reclaiming attention "without renegotiation." This is not conservatism; it is the dependency structure of the framing itself — the two stakeholders who could license the automation readings are not in the room, the baseline detection rate is unmeasured, and the chosen design is the only one whose value is unconditional on all three.

## Trade-offs accepted

1. **The latency ceiling is accepted as a consequence, not a risk.** The organization accepts that the routine cohort keeps a human decision in its path and that mean wait may not fall — and may rise modestly under the batch-cadence floor — for this generation. This is the price of preserving guarantee (a) in per-change form and of delivering without Q1's license. It is accepted because the framing's own metric is attention quality (D2), not hours; it is monitored, not assumed (E5), so if the sponsor's retained objective turns out to be wait-time after all, the data says so and the branch is the remedy.
2. **The A5 bet is accepted.** The organization accepts that this design's safety increment — the claim that better-allocated attention improves screening — rests on an assumption the framing flags as unevidenced, in exchange for a control that cannot be weakened by mechanism and cannot fail open. The bet is accepted *because it is testable in P0 data before any queue change ships* (E1): if it fails, the honest conclusion is recorded in advance — the automation designs' machine-checkable safety boundaries would deserve their weight, and this design's upside was always the assumption.
3. **The reorganization cost is accepted.** Scheduled batch sessions, new reviewer workflows, and a formal queue-operations role change how the fixed pool works and consume reviewer calendar. The organization accepts the management price of reallocation because reallocation cannot be produced by machinery; the alternative to paying it is pretending the coordination is free, which is how today's queue coordination silently fails.
4. **Automation is deferred but not abandoned, and the deferral is accepted.** The organization accepts spending this generation on instrumentation and attention reallocation rather than decision automation, with the branch specified and gated on the security conversation. A favorable Q1 answer later means the velocity win arrives a generation late; that lateness is accepted as the price of not modifying a named guarantee on an unasked question.

## Designs considered and not chosen

- **Classify-and-auto-approve.** Not chosen now; named as the specified automation branch. Not chosen because its live mode is conditional on five unanswered or unowned items (Q1, Q4, Q2 values, Q6, Q9), because it modifies guarantee (a) before the license exists, and because its shadow cutover criterion is its own conceded weakness (agreement with a rubber-stamping oracle cannot be distinguished from correct agreement). Its decision-record machinery, provenance taxonomy, conjunction, and determinism are adopted as shared infrastructure and preserved for the branch — a strength the compaction records rather than erases.
- **Policy-replaces-approval.** Not chosen. Its certificate semantics cannot exist until Q10 is answered (the guarantee list is unratified and the approvers unasked — its own I1); its accountability model for decisions nobody actuated is nominal by its own admission (F2); it self-reports as the heaviest consumer of unsecured security-team time (D6); and its platform ask is the sharpest Q4 reading (a stage bypass). Its encodability tracker — the one genuinely new instrument it contributes — is adopted here in byproduct form through escalation records and anchored dispositions.
- **Idea 3, advisory-only variant** (tier/brief shown with no reallocation). Not chosen: information without reallocation lands in the queue shape that manufactures rubber-stamping, which is why the framing itself dismissed it as inert. The advisory content is adopted as the brief; the allocation layer is the design's work, not a garnish.
- **Idea 3, default-approve-with-a-window.** Not chosen, and rejected hardest: a de facto automation that inherits every Q1/Q5/Q6 dependency of the automation directions while keeping none of their audit machinery, whose decision is a non-event (silence becomes consent, an approval without a name), which is precisely the unauditable non-decision D14 rejects.
- **Sub-variants rejected within the chosen direction:** batch-level sign-off (violates guarantee (b) per deployment), enforced routing (presentation becomes a gate, importing Q4), mechanical enqueue blockers (a new control with its own override problem — replaced by non-binding screening plus measurement), learned ML tiering from day one (no labels exist; the design generates them), score-based tiering (a number to interpret instead of the failed-predicate list that routes attention).

## Unresolved questions for experimentation

Questions a stakeholder must answer — the security license (Q1), the platform team's no-side-channel interpretation and change-process gate (Q4, Q11), the guarantee list's completeness (Q10), the emergency-revert design (Q7) — are deliberately not listed here: no experiment can answer them. They remain gates on the automation branch and are carried in Implementation constraints. The following are the empirical questions whose answers materially change what should ship:

**E1. Does better-prepared, differentiated attention improve screening quality (A5)?** *Hypothesis:* brief-assisted review at right-sized depth detects denials/rework at least as well as today's undifferentiated review, per change class. *Experiment:* correlate review-context records (brief version, tier, time) with disposition outcomes in P0/P1 data; compare per-class denial/rework detection before vs. after brief presentation; track escalation confirmations (do escalated items produce denials/rework at elevated rates). *Success signal:* per-class denial detection holds or improves as routine review time falls. *Failure signal:* brief-assisted low-time reviews miss denials that deep re-review finds — the framing's A5 fails its test and the automation branch's evidence picture changes.

**E2. Can a conservative mechanical conjunction isolate a routine-shaped bulk worth batching?** *Hypothesis:* the T1 predicate set, starting at the mechanically provable core, captures a material share of deployment volume with near-zero escalation-confirmed mis-tiering. *Experiment:* run the tierer in recording mode for 4–6 weeks; measure T1 volume share, escalation rate, escalation-confirmation rate. *Success signal:* T1 ≥ ~30% of volume with escalation-confirmed consequential mis-tiering below a low queue-ops threshold. *Failure signal:* T1 is tiny (no bulk to batch) or escalations repeatedly confirm consequential changes were batch-screened — either way, tiering policy revises conservatively before any batching ships.

**E3. Does enqueue screening cut rework cycles without forming a social gate?** *Hypothesis:* deployers shown the anchored mechanical failures their review would face stop enqueueing doomed changes. *Experiment:* track flagged-but-enqueued rate and denial/rework rate on flagged items before/after screening ships; watch for F7's collapse-to-zero. *Success signal:* rework rate on enqueued items falls while flagged-but-enqueued stays bounded above zero. *Failure signal:* no rework reduction (screening inert) or flagged-but-enqueued collapses (the prediction became an undocumented gate — a policy/communication intervention, not a continuation).

**E4. Does cohort batching compress per-item review time without degrading disposition quality?** *Hypothesis:* warm context plus pre-loaded briefs cuts per-item time materially while per-item disposition records hold quality. *Experiment:* feature-flag batching on two domains during P2; compare, within the same reviewers, time-per-item, anchored-denial share, and downstream rework between batched and unbatched T1 items. *Success signal:* time-per-item down meaningfully with anchored disposition share and rework unchanged. *Failure signal:* batch blur — anchored denials fall or rework rises on batched items, or time does not move.

**E5. What does differentiated operation do to routine-class queue delay?** *Hypothesis:* predictable batch cadence plus fewer rework re-entries offsets the cadence floor; routine wait does not materially worsen while attention metrics improve. *Experiment:* attention-ledger queue-age by tier before and during P2, against the P0 baseline. *Success signal:* routine-class p95 wait within a small tolerance of baseline or better. *Failure signal:* material degradation with no attention-quality gain — the accepted latency trade-off (Trade-off 1) is then failing its own test and batching cadence or class scoping must be revised.

**E6. Do anchored dispositions produce labels usable by a future classifier (the on-ramp's feedstock)?** *Hypothesis:* structured, rule-anchored disposition records stay informative enough to serve future automation. *Experiment:* monthly independent sample of denial/rework disposition records scored informative/uninformative; track anchored-share and empty-reason rate (F8 watch). *Success signal:* informed share ≥ ~80% sustained. *Failure signal:* reason fatigue — boilerplate rot rising, anchored share falling — in which case label quality must be treated as a first-class maintenance burden before the branch can consume its feed.

## Confidence

Confidence in this proposed design: **0.65**. The design is coherent, deliverable on the organization's current staffing, preserves all three named guarantees unmodified, cannot fail open, and degrades to exactly the status quo; its dependency structure removes the framing's largest unknown (the security conversation) from the critical path while preserving a recorded, gated path to automation. The largest source of uncertainty is the framing's own unevidenced assumption A5 — that attention quality actually serves screening quality — now load-bearing for this design's safety increment and its success metric, with reviewer adoption (F10) as the second. Both are testable in the first data phase (E1, E2), which is why confidence is 0.65 rather than lower: the design's central uncertainty is exactly what its first phase is built to measure.
